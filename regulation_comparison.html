01 / GOVERNANCE

Governance & Scope of the Regulations

AR-23 · Raghu Engineering College

AR-23 is an institutional regulation document for an autonomous engineering college affiliated to JNTU-GV, Vizianagaram. The document explicitly governs the B.Tech programme from the batch admitted in academic year 2023-24 onwards, with a parallel section for lateral-entry students from 2024-25 onwards.

Approval and amendment authority sits with the Vice-Chancellor of the affiliating University; the College Academic Committee handles operational matters like attendance condonation. The regulations are explicitly framed around AICTE-recommended credit distributions and APSCHE guidelines.

FFCS v4.0 · VIT

FFCS v4.0 is a deemed-university-wide regulation that applies to all programmes (B.Tech, M.Tech, MCA, MBA, M.Sc., BBA, BCA, B.Des, B.Arch, etc.) at VIT, not just engineering. It applies to students admitted from 2021-22 onwards.

The regulations have a documented version history dating back to FFCS 1.0 (2009), with each revision tied to an Academic Council meeting number and date. Version 4.0 was approved at the 72nd Academic Council meeting on 13 December 2023. Final interpretive authority lies with the Academic Council.

AR-23 is a single-programme regulation (B.Tech only) for an affiliated autonomous college, written tightly around AICTE/APSCHE prescriptions. FFCS v4.0 is a university-wide framework covering UG and PG across disciplines, with explicit version control and a research-university orientation.

AR-23

To earn the degree the student must (a) pursue the programme for not less than 4 and not more than 8 academic years and (b) register for and secure all 160 credits. Honours requires an additional 15 credits taken simultaneously with the B.Tech.

Students failing to complete in 8 years forfeit their seat and admission stands cancelled.

FFCS v4.0

Minimum durations are listed for every degree the university offers (B.Tech: 4 yrs, M.Tech: 2 yrs, M.Tech-Integrated: 5 yrs, MCA/MBA/M.Sc: 2 yrs, BBA/BCA/B.Sc/B.Com: 3 yrs, M.Sc-Integrated: 5 yrs, B.Arch: 5 yrs). Provisional certificate is issued by the CoE once requirements are met; the degree is conferred at the next Convocation.

Degree certificate text follows a precise format that includes Minor/Honours specializations: e.g. "Bachelor of Technology (Honours) in Computer Science Engineering with specialization in Data Science."

Both regulations cap the programme to roughly the same horizon, but the philosophies differ. AR-23 talks of "forfeiting the seat" — a harder line. FFCS v4.0 uses a softer "studentship stands cancelled automatically" formula but adds explicit provision for break of study (1 year, once per programme) and a documented "gap year" pathway through the affiliating university for AR-23 students.

03 / ADMISSION

Admission Pathways

AR-23

Admissions are governed by AP State Government / University rules. Selection is by merit rank in the common entrance examination (effectively EAPCET) conducted by the AP government, or any other government-approved merit order, subject to state-prescribed reservations.

The college itself has no admission entrance test; it operates inside the AP government counseling framework.

FFCS v4.0

VIT conducts its own national-level computer-based entrance exams: VITEEE for B.Tech, VITMEE for M.Tech. Admission is through university counseling on the merit list of these exams.

For programmes outside the engineering vertical (BBA, B.Sc., etc.), admission is on merit plus programme-specific criteria as published in the annual Admissions brochure.

State-government counseling (AR-23) versus university-run national entrance exam (FFCS) — the structural difference between an affiliated autonomous college and a deemed-to-be university under section 3 of the UGC Act.

04 / CALENDAR

Academic Calendar & Semester Structure

AR-23
  • Two semesters per year (one odd + one even).
  • Minimum 90 working days per semester, including exams.
  • A summer term of 8 weeks for internships/apprenticeships, particularly for students who exit after 2 or 4 semesters.
  • 3-week mandatory induction before Semester 1, per AICTE template.
  • Preferably 25% of each theory course is taught in blended-learning mode.
FFCS v4.0
  • Two regular semesters: Fall (Jul–Nov) and Winter (Dec–May).
  • 15 instructional non-exam weeks per semester (explicit clock-hours per credit).
  • Optional Weekend Intra Semester and Summer Semester in fast-track mode for clearing backlogs — not binding on the university.
  • Slot-based timetable: students build their own time-table from open slots; classmates may have entirely different schedules.

AR-23 follows a conventional fixed timetable with summer used mostly for industry exposure. FFCS introduces slot-based scheduling with optional weekend and summer terms, fundamentally enabling personalised academic pacing — the defining philosophical move of the "Fully Flexible" Credit System.

AR-23

Eight clearly labelled buckets: HM BS ES PC PE OE SEC PR MC.

Specific count: 5 Professional Electives + 4 Open Electives + 5 Skill-Oriented Courses. Of the 5 skill courses: 2 from the same domain, 1 soft skill, 2 advanced skill (interdisciplinary). The total open-elective count enables an in-curriculum Minor.

Both use roughly similar buckets, but FFCS adds finer granularity: Specialization Elective (restricted basket) is treated separately from Discipline Elective (broader); Ability Enhancement sits alongside Skill Enhancement; and courses come encoded with cognitive levels and versions. AR-23 sticks to the AICTE-mandated bucket percentages almost verbatim.

AR-23

AR-23 spells out credit weights for L/T/P as a simple weekly conversion table. Courses are written in L-T-P-C format, exactly as AICTE recommends. Half-credits (e.g. 0.5 for one practical hour) appear in the structure.

FFCS v4.0

FFCS defines a credit as 15 hours of L or T, or 30 hours of P, executed over a 15-week semester. Every course has a 4-tuple LTPC; this maps directly to instructional minutes per week (50-min units for L/T, 100-min units for P).

FFCS expresses the credit rule as total instructional hours per credit (15 L/T or 30 P) — useful because some courses are taught in fast-track mode in summer/weekend semesters where the per-week count rises but the totals stay constant. AR-23 sticks to a strict per-week formula, which assumes a regular semester only.

07 / MINIMUM CREDITS

Minimum Credit Requirement for Degree

AR-23

Hard requirement: 160 credits for B.Tech (Regular), 120 credits for Lateral Entry, plus all FC/PC courses to be cleared individually. An additional 15 credits for B.Tech (Honors).

No category-level relaxation is granted — every course listed in the structure of the chosen branch must be cleared.

FFCS v4.0

Minimum credits are programme-specific and declared in each curriculum. Critically: it is possible under FFCS to hit the total credit number without meeting category-level minima — and in that case the student must still complete all FC/DLE/DC courses and the required quanta in OE/DE/SE/SEC/AEC.

Minor and Honours each demand 18 additional credits with a minimum average CGPA of 7.50 in those courses.

AR-23's count (160) is fixed for any B.Tech branch; FFCS is programme-defined. Additional credentials differ in scale: AR-23 Honours needs +15 credits, FFCS Minor/Honours each demand +18 credits with a CGPA condition layered on top.

08 / REGISTRATION

Course Registration Process

AR-23

AR-23 follows a more conventional model: a student takes the courses listed for their semester in their branch's course-structure document. The structure for I-Year I-Sem and II-Sem is identical for every student in the branch. Registration is implicit — students pursue what their branch prescribes.

Skill courses give students a limited choice (institutional offering vs. external certification — see §17). MOOCs allow some external substitution.

FFCS v4.0

Formal annual Course Registration process. Each student picks courses (and slots) for the coming semester. A Proctor (faculty advisor) reviews the plan against curriculum, prerequisites, and academic performance before approving it. The system supports late registration (within 10 calendar days) with a late fee.

First semester is auto-registered by the School Academic Coordinator; from semester 2 onwards the student drives the choice.

This is the biggest philosophical difference between the two regulations. AR-23 places the student inside a fixed semester structure with limited choice. FFCS makes course-by-course, slot-by-slot registration the central mechanic of student agency — and assigns a proctor to guide it.

09 / ADD-DROP

Add/Drop & Course Withdrawal

AR-23

AR-23 contains no explicit add/drop or course-withdrawal provision. Since the semester course list is essentially fixed per branch, the question doesn't arise in the same way.

Students who can't continue may seek re-admission when the semester is re-offered (§24, Transitory Regulations) but this means dropping out of the cohort, not a structured withdrawal.

FFCS v4.0
  • Add/Drop window: first 3 instructional days of every regular semester. Dropped courses don't appear on the Grade Sheet.
  • Course Withdrawal: open for 3 instructional days after CAT-I marks are finalised, if the student feels CAT-I performance is poor. Withdrawn course gets a W grade, listed on academic history but not on Grade Sheets or transcripts. Re-registration fee applies.
  • Backlog re-registered courses cannot be withdrawn.
  • Withdrawal can't take the credit-load below 16 (final-year and timed-out students exempted).

FFCS gives students a structured exit ramp from a course that's going badly — a notable safety valve missing from AR-23, where the only way to "withdraw" is to fail and re-take.

10 / PREREQ

Course Prerequisites & Equivalence

AR-23

The regulation cautions students against opting for electives with content that overlaps courses already pursued, but does not formalise prerequisite/anti-requisite/co-requisite chains. Course equivalence is handled implicitly when the curriculum is revised (existing batches continue on the curriculum they joined under).

FFCS v4.0

FFCS formalises three relationships per course: prerequisite, co-requisite, anti-requisite. A student with an F in a prerequisite is still allowed to register the higher-level course, on the principle that "exposure" was achieved.

Course equivalence is a first-class concept: a Course Committee can declare two courses equivalent (typically across versions or curriculum revisions), which then auto-updates baskets across all affected curricula. The original course code/title is preserved on the transcript.

AR-23

Both an aggregate (75% across all subjects) and a per-subject (40%) requirement must be met. A condonation band of 65–74% aggregate is available, on payment of a fee to the University. Below 65% aggregate is non-condonable and the student is not promoted to the next semester.

FFCS v4.0

Per-course 75% with no condonation. The attendance software auto-debars students from the next exam component (CAT-I, CAT-II, FAT) as soon as the threshold is breached, and a "debarred" student is awarded an N grade and must re-register.

A unique perk: students with CGPA ≥ 9.0 and no current backlog are exempted from minimum attendance — and the list is generated dynamically before each exam component.

AR-23 is more forgiving (condonation with fee); FFCS is more automated (software-driven debarring) but rewards top performers by exempting them entirely. Both target the same 75% norm.

AR-23

Two mid-term exams. Each is 30 marks total = 10 (objective, 20 min) + 15 (subjective, 90 min) + 5 (assignment-average). First mid covers Units I & II; second covers Units III, IV, V. The final mid mark is a weighted blend: 0.8 × better mid + 0.2 × weaker mid.

If absent: no re-exam; that mid is recorded as zero (used as the weaker mid in the blend).

FFCS v4.0

CAT-I (15 marks weight) + CAT-II (15 marks weight, open-book) + Digital Assignments (30 marks weight; min. 3 assignments OR alternative continuous assessment) = 60% CAM. The faculty announces the assessment mode and weightages before the course starts, with HoD approval.

Re-CATs are organised for students absent due to internship abroad with permission, fellowship work, placement activities, or hospitalisation.

FFCS gives twice the weight to internal work (60% vs 30%) — a deliberate design choice to reward sustained engagement over a single end-of-semester exam. FFCS also makes CAT-II open-book by default, requiring 100% Higher-Order-Thinking questions in that test. AR-23 leans more heavily on the end exam.

13 / END EXAM

End-Semester / Final Assessment Exam — Theory

AR-23

End exam is for 70 marks, 3 hours. Structure:

  • Q1: 10 compulsory short-answer questions × 2 marks = 20 marks (two from each unit).
  • Q2–Q6: one "either/or" question per unit, 10 marks each (50 marks total).

For two-part courses (e.g., BEEE), the question paper is split into Part A and Part B of 35 marks each.

FFCS v4.0

Final Assessment Test (FAT) for 100 marks, 3 hours; weighted at 40% of the final grade. Question pattern is not fixed in the regulation but governed by Course Committees. Minimum 80% of questions must be HOTs-based.

FAT is conducted centrally by the CoE office; CATs are conducted by the School.

AR-23's end exam has a tightly mandated, almost prescriptive structure (specific number of compulsory questions, unit-by-unit coverage, either/or pattern). FFCS leaves the structure to faculty/Course Committees but mandates the HOTs proportion.

15 / EMBEDDED

Embedded Theory + Lab Courses

AR-23

If a course has both theory and practical components, they are evaluated separately as a theory subject and a practical subject, but with the same course code suffixed by 'T' and 'P'. Each has its own 30+70 split, its own grade, its own pass criterion.

FFCS v4.0

Embedded courses have a single combined grade. Marks ratio: (L+T) : (P/2). To pass the embedded course the student must score ≥50% in the lab component and ≥40% in the theory FAT and ≥50% overall.

If the student fails only the lab, they re-register just the lab component; if they pass lab but fail theory, lab marks carry forward to the re-attempt.

AR-23: splits the course into independent T/P modules with separate grades. FFCS: keeps them fused with a single overall grade but allows partial re-registration. The FFCS model preserves the integrative spirit of the course; AR-23 keeps things modular and granular.

16 / PROJECT

Project & Internship

AR-23

Two summer internships are compulsory, each ≥8 weeks:

  • After Year 2: Community Service Project (society-oriented, with govt/NGOs).
  • After Year 3: Industry Internship.

Each summer internship is evaluated for 50 marks (external only, no internal): 50% on report, 50% on oral presentation before a departmental committee.

Final-semester Full Internship + Project Work: 200 marks total = 60 internal (15 report + 15 seminar by supervisor + 30 dept Project Review Committee) + 140 external viva-voce.

FFCS v4.0

Industrial Internship: 4 weeks in any reputed industry after Year 1, can be in one stretch or two 2-week blocks. Activity log + report + completion certificate; School committee awards marks.

Final-Semester Project: structured 3-review system —

  1. Review 1 (5%): title acceptance, Week 1 of semester.
  2. Review 2 (40%): scheduled during CAT-I.
  3. Review 3 / Final Viva (50%): during FAT period; 25% report (guide) + 25% viva (external).
  4. Draft submission (5%): 10 days before viva.

Group projects up to 5 members; individual report titles must reflect individual contributions. Submitting a purchased / borrowed / previously-submitted project = malpractice → N grade.

AR-23 emphasizes a community-service internship (society-oriented) as a separate mandatory step — a feature absent from FFCS. FFCS, conversely, has a much more granular review/timeline framework for the final project with named review weights and submission deadlines. AR-23 internship totals 50 marks; FFCS does not pin a specific mark figure but uses the review weightages.

17 / SKILL

Skill-Oriented Courses

AR-23

5 skill-oriented courses across semesters III–VII. Mix: 2 domain skill + 2 advanced skill/interdisciplinary + 1 soft-skill. Each 100 marks (30 CIE + 70 end-exam).

A unique option: students may swap any skill course for an industry/professional-body certification. The principal sends such proposals to the affiliating University for grade-equivalence approval.

FFCS v4.0

Skill Enhancement Courses (SEC) and Ability Enhancement Courses (AEC) are listed as separate categories. Students choose from these baskets per the curriculum. Evaluation pattern follows the same theory/lab/embedded rules.

External certification is not a generalised substitution option; rather, certain credits can flow in via SWAYAM-NPTEL MOOCs against Open Elective slots.

18 / MOOCs

MOOCs & Credit Transfer

AR-23

One MOOC is mandatory for the degree, with minimum 8 or 12 weeks of duration. Core (PC) courses are not allowed to be taken via MOOC. The HoD appoints a mentor; the student bears any exam fee.

The University permits MOOC-based credit transfer up to 20% of total programme credits (max ≈ 32 credits), applicable to Professional and Open electives only. Students with proctored MOOC exam passes are exempted from internal and external exams for those courses.

FFCS v4.0

SWAYAM-NPTEL MOOCs are permitted, but only as credit transfer against Open Elective slots — not against DE, DC, FC, or DLE. The credit-transfer regime overall is well-defined for inter-university migration (programme migration within VIT, or admission from another institution under recognised arrangements).

FFCS does not mandate a MOOC for graduation — it's an optional pathway.

AR-23 makes one MOOC compulsory for the degree and allows up to 20% of credits through MOOCs — strongly aligned with NEP 2020 blended-learning targets. FFCS treats MOOCs as a supplementary credit-transfer channel restricted to Open Electives, not as a mandate.

19 / ABC

Academic Bank of Credits (ABC)

AR-23

AR-23 has an explicit Section 14 on the Academic Bank of Credits: the University has implemented ABC per NEP 2020 to (i) enable mobility across universities, (ii) enable MOOC credits to flow in, (iii) underpin the certificate/diploma/degree awards for multi-entry-exit, (iv) maintain credit accounts in the student's ABC ID.

FFCS v4.0

FFCS v4.0 does not formalise ABC as a named section. Credit transfer is handled through programme migration rules and the Semester Abroad Program — internal mechanisms rather than the national-bank framework.

AR-23 is more explicitly NEP-2020-aligned on ABC; FFCS handles inter-institutional credit-mobility through its own credit-transfer framework but is silent on the national ABC platform in the regulation document.

AR-23

Pure absolute grading. A student passes a course if they secure (i) ≥35% in the end exam and (ii) ≥40% in CIE + EE combined. For mandatory non-credit courses, the threshold is 40% of internal marks.

'AB' grade for absentees (carries 0 pts and is treated as failed). Audit/satisfactory courses are marked S/U but excluded from SGPA/CGPA.

FFCS v4.0

Pass conditions: (a) ≥40% in theory FAT, (b) ≥50% in lab/project components, (c) ≥50% overall in any course except theory-only — and (d) for theory-only the pass band is derived from the relative-grading curve.

Grades: S/A/B/C/D/E/F/N/W/U/P. N grade is uniquely FFCS — assigned when a component is incomplete (not the same as F).

FFCS sets the bar higher — minimum overall is 50%, versus AR-23's 40%. FFCS also has the unique N grade (incomplete; component-level) distinct from F (fail), enabling partial re-registration. AR-23 has no equivalent "N" — students either pass, fail, or are absent.

21 / SGPA-CGPA

SGPA / CGPA / GPA Calculation

AR-23

SGPA = Σ(Ci × Gi) / ΣCi. CGPA = Σ(Ci × Si) / ΣCi — where Si is the SGPA of the i-th semester.

Zero-graded courses are included while computing SGPA. Audit/non-credit courses are excluded. CGPA-to-% formula: (CGPA − 0.5) × 10.

Both SGPA and CGPA are reported to two decimals.

FFCS v4.0

CGPA = Σ(Ci × GPi) / ΣCi. SGPA computed per semester likewise.

Pass-Fail (P), audit, Minor and Honours courses are excluded. If a course is failed multiple times or substituted, credits count once in both numerator and denominator. CGPA-to-% formula: CGPA × 10 (8.75 CGPA ≡ 87.5%).

The Consolidated Grade Sheet shows only CGPA — no class or division label.

Both use the standard weighted-average formula but with very different percentage conversions. AR-23 deducts 0.5 first (so 8.75 CGPA = 82.5%); FFCS multiplies straight (so 8.75 CGPA = 87.5%). For students applying abroad or to government jobs requiring percentage equivalence, this 5-point gap is material.

22 / RELATIVE GRADING

Absolute vs Relative Grading

AR-23

AR-23 uses purely absolute grading. Letter grades map to fixed mark ranges (≥90 = S, 80–89 = A, etc.) regardless of class performance.

FFCS v4.0

For theory components with class strength > 10, FFCS uses class-wise relative grading. The class mean is anchored to mid-B; standard deviations σ above/below set the grade bands:

  • S: ≥ mean+1.5σ (also ≥90%)
  • A: mean+0.5σ to mean+1.5σ
  • B: mean−0.5σ to mean+0.5σ
  • C: mean−1σ to mean−0.5σ
  • D: mean−1.5σ to mean−1σ
  • E: mean−2σ to mean−1.5σ
  • F: < mean−2σ

Lab-only, project, soft-skill, NGCR, and small-class (≤10) courses use the absolute table above (§20).

This is the single most consequential difference in evaluation philosophy. Under FFCS relative grading, a student's letter grade depends on how their peers performed; under AR-23 it depends only on their own marks. FFCS argues this neutralises "easy/hard paper" effects; AR-23's absolute system is more transparent and predictable.

23 / PROMOTION

Promotion Rules

AR-23
  • I → II: attendance norms met.
  • II → III: at least 40% of the credits studied through Sem III (rounded down).
  • III → IV: at least 40% of credits studied through Sem V (rounded down).
  • Detained students may clear deficits via supplementary exams before joining V or VII sem.
FFCS v4.0

Promotion is automatic — irrespective of academic performance. However, if a student's CGPA dips below 4.00, they are capped at 16 credits per semester until CGPA recovers. Repeated failure to recover triggers a Dean-of-Academics committee review which can recommend cancellation of studentship to the VC.

AR-23 uses credit-based promotion gates (must clear 40% of credits to move up); FFCS uses automatic promotion with a credit-load throttle for academically struggling students. The latter avoids the risk of a student being "stuck" in a year but adds explicit safety-net machinery.

24 / RE-EVAL

Re-evaluation & Answer-Sheet Perusal

AR-23

AR-23 does not detail a re-evaluation process within the regulation text supplied. Standard university procedures apply, and lab record/mid papers are preserved for at least 3 years at the institution for university inspection.

FFCS v4.0

A three-tier process:

  1. Perusal: student sees the FAT script (after paying fee).
  2. Re-evaluation: if marks shift ≤5%, best of two stands. If shift >5%, a third examiner evaluates and best of three stands.
  3. Examination Appellate Committee: final step; original evaluator + an expert from the Course Committee re-evaluate together. Decision is final.

If a class average exceeds 90%, the FAT scripts are automatically re-evaluated by a second examiner; the original faculty must explain the high average.

No re-evaluation for lab exams, soft skills, project viva, design/mini-project courses, or comprehensive exams.

25 / EXAM ABSENCE

Absence from Exams & Re-FAT

AR-23

Mid-exam absence: no re-exam; marks recorded as zero. Final marks computed using the weighted blend (80/20) with the absent test as the weaker one (zero).

End-exam absence: 'AB' grade (0 pts) → re-register / supplementary exam.

FFCS v4.0

Re-CAT: organised separately for students absent due to (a) approved international internship, (b) Indian Academy fellowships, (c) placement activities, (d) hospitalisation — provided attendance criterion is met.

Re-FAT: students absent for the FAT due to hospitalization (in-patient, > 1 week) or death in family may apply via Dean to the VC within 14 calendar days, with proper medical certificate. A special committee scrutinises and may grant a Re-FAT — treated as first attempt. Failure to write the Re-FAT means the original N grade stands.

FFCS has a formal compassionate-exam pathway (Re-CAT, Re-FAT) with explicit triggers and timelines. AR-23 takes a stricter line — missed mids are zeros, missed end-exams require re-registration.

26 / BACKLOG

Backlog & Re-registration

AR-23

Failed courses → supplementary exams when the semester is offered. There is no separate "re-registration of a single component" — the student re-appears for the end exam (mid marks carry over). Detained students may make up credits through supplementary exams.

FFCS v4.0

Subtle but powerful distinction between F and N:

  • F (failed) → must re-register the whole course (all components reset).
  • N (incomplete) → may re-register only the failed component; passed components carry forward.

If a student has F in a prerequisite course they can still register the next level course (the "exposure" doctrine). Backlogs can also be cleared via arrear examinations after the final semester, but this requires re-appearing for all components, not just the failed ones.

Backlog courses are also offered in summer/weekend semesters where feasible.

27 / GRADE IMPROVEMENT

Grade Improvement

AR-23

AR-23 does not contain a formal grade-improvement option for already-passed courses.

FFCS v4.0

Students may re-register a passed course to improve the grade, paying the registration fee. Limit: once per course, one course per semester (relaxed for final-year/timed-out students to help placement CGPA). The course must be tagged "Grade Improvement" during registration. Credit cap (16–27 per regular semester) is not relaxed.

Grade improvement is unique to FFCS — a clear student-friendly tool to lift CGPA, especially valuable for placement and graduate-school applications.

28 / SUBSTITUTION

Course Substitution

AR-23

Not formally provided. Failed PE/OE courses must be retaken; the regulations do warn students against picking electives that overlap with prior courses.

FFCS v4.0

If a student fails a DE, they may substitute another DE from the same basket in a subsequent semester. If a student fails an OE, they may substitute another OE — or even a DE — in its place. Re-registration fee applies. Substitution is not allowed for FC, DC, or DLE.

29 / AUDIT

Audit Courses

AR-23

Non-credit mandatory courses (Environmental Sciences, Indian Constitution, Tech Paper Writing & IPR) are marked S/U and excluded from SGPA/CGPA. AR-23 has no formal "Audit" option for picking new optional courses beyond these mandatory ones.

FFCS v4.0

Students with CGPA ≥ 8.0 may register up to 2 audit courses (B.Tech/M.Tech-Integrated/M.Sc-Integrated) or 1 audit course (others) during the entire programme. Audit courses appear on the transcript with a U grade (if performance grade earned). All attendance and assessment rules apply. The course doesn't count to SGPA/CGPA.

If audited but failed/withdrawn, the course is shown as W in the Semester Grade Sheet and removed from the Consolidated Grade Sheet.

AR-23 — registration

Students who maintain CGPA ≥ 7.5 with no backlogs through Sem III register Honours from Sem IV onwards. Max 2 Honours courses per semester from Sem V onwards. 75% attendance on Honours courses is mandatory; attendance is tracked independently of the Major.

If detained or attendance fails on Honours → Honours registration cancelled, but Major continues.

FFCS — registration

Honours runs through normal Course Registration alongside the Major. The student accumulates credits across semesters; once 18 credits + CGPA ≥7.5 in Honours basket is achieved within programme time-limit, the credential is awarded.

If the student withdraws or fails, completed Honours courses are converted to Audit on the transcript.

AR-23 enforces an upfront CGPA gate (≥7.5) for entry and rations Honours load (max 2/sem). FFCS imposes the CGPA bar at completion and allows the student to attempt as many Honours courses as the 27-credit per-semester cap allows. Both name the credential identically on the degree certificate.

FFCS — Class Awarded

FFCS explicitly states: the Consolidated Grade Sheet and Degree Certificate do not mention any class — only the CGPA is shown. Programme-wise rank lists are prepared on CGPA alone (with shared rank in case of ties; next student gets the skipped rank).

This is a striking philosophical difference. AR-23 retains the classical First Class with Distinction / First / Second / Pass system and ties Distinction to a "no supplementary attempts" purity condition. FFCS abolishes class designations and lets the CGPA speak for itself.

33 / EXIT

Multiple Entry & Exit (NEP 2020)

AR-23

Three structured exit options as per NEP 2020:

  • UG Certificate — after Year 1 (40 credits + 10-credit bridge with ≥6-cr internship).
  • UG Diploma — after Year 2 (80 credits + 10-credit bridge).
  • B.Sc. Engineering — after Year 3 (120 credits).

Re-entry modalities are stated to be defined in due course.

FFCS v4.0

FFCS v4.0 does not provide a formal multi-entry-multi-exit framework. The regulation focuses on full programme completion. Programme migration between programmes (within VIT) is allowed at the end of Year 1, but this is internal, not a national NEP exit.

AR-23 is significantly more aligned with the NEP 2020 multiple-entry-multiple-exit doctrine; FFCS v4.0 has not yet adopted it in the regulation text.

34 / GAP YEAR

Gap Year / Break of Study

AR-23

"Student Entrepreneur in Residence" gap year — outstanding students may take a break of 1 year (extendable to 2) after Year 2 to pursue entrepreneurship or set up startups. The 2 years are not counted against the 8-year max programme duration. Proposals are forwarded by the Principal to a University-level evaluation committee.

FFCS v4.0

Break of Study — up to 1 year for valid reasons (accident, prolonged hospitalisation). Recommended by School Dean, approved by Dean Academics, ordered by Registrar. The time limit is extended by the break period. Available once per programme. Indiscipline-based debarring does not count as break of study.

AR-23's gap year is entrepreneurship-focused — explicitly a build-your-startup year. FFCS's break of study is medical/personal. Both extend the maximum duration; neither stacks indefinitely.

35 / MANDATORY

Mandatory / Non-Credit Courses

AR-23
  • Environmental Sciences, Indian Constitution, Technical Paper Writing & IPR — non-credit mandatory.
  • Health and Wellness, Yoga & Sports / NSS / NCC / Scouts & Guides — credit-mandatory (0.5 credit each).
  • Design Thinking for Innovation & Tinkering Labs — credit-mandatory.

For non-credit mandatory courses there is no external exam — but attendance counts toward aggregate and the student must score ≥40% in internals to pass. If fail, a re-exam for 30 marks is offered.

FFCS v4.0

FFCS uses Non-Graded Core Requirement (NGCR) and similar tags for compulsory non-credit courses; specific lists are programme-specific. There is no equivalent fixed list of "Constitution + IPR + Environmental" in the FFCS regulation document — the curriculum determines it.

Bridge courses (assigned based on a screening test in Sem 1) are also mandatory but excluded from CGPA.

36 / SAP

Semester Abroad & International Exposure

AR-23

AR-23 does not formalise a Semester Abroad Program. Students may participate in MOOCs from international platforms (within the 20% credit-transfer cap), but a structured one-semester exchange is not described.

FFCS v4.0

A full Semester Abroad Program (SAP) is detailed:

  • Eligibility: CGPA ≥ 7.0, no current backlogs.
  • Applicable institutes: VIT partner universities + Top-500 QS / THE / Shanghai ranked.
  • Duration: 1 semester (4–5 months) — extendable to 2 semesters.
  • Max transferable credits: 21 credits (plus project credits separately).
  • Credit transfer is restricted to DE/PE and OE/UE categories.
  • Grades from abroad count toward VIT CGPA.
  • Final project can be done abroad under a foreign guide (Option I) or through internship-cum-project (Option II).

The window varies by programme — see FFCS Table 7. B.Tech students may avail it in Sem 6, 7, or 8.

37 / MALPRACTICE

Academic Malpractice

AR-23

The regulation states "Malpractices rules-nature and punishments are appended" — but the appendix is not part of the document supplied. Generally, malpractice cases are governed by the affiliating University's malpractice rules.

FFCS v4.0

A formal Malpractice Committee enquires every reported case.

  • Malpractice in a CAT → zero marks awarded in all subjects registered in that semester for that CAT component (across-the-board penalty).
  • Malpractice in the FAT → N grade in all regular courses for which the student was eligible to write and attended the FAT that semester. Courses must be cleared via re-registration.
  • Submitting a purchased/borrowed/repeated project → exam malpractice → N grade.

FFCS is unusually severe — malpractice in one paper can wipe out an entire semester's exam component. AR-23 defers to the affiliating university's malpractice schedule.

38 / WITHHOLDING

Withholding of Results

AR-23

Results are withheld if the candidate has dues unpaid to the university, or if any case of indiscipline or malpractice is pending.

FFCS v4.0

No standalone "withholding of results" section, but registration itself is blocked when dues are outstanding (the student can register for courses but not attend classes until dues are cleared).

39 / MEDIUM

Medium of Instruction

AR-23

"The medium of instruction of the entire B.Tech undergraduate programme in Engineering & Technology (including examinations and project reports) will be in English only."

FFCS v4.0

FFCS does not explicitly state the medium in the supplied text — English is implicit across all programmes. Foreign Language courses are explicitly accommodated within the FC basket.

40 / TRANSITORY

Transitory / Re-admission Rules

AR-23

Discontinued/detained/failed candidates are eligible for re-admission when the semester is offered again. They follow the academic regulations into which they are re-admitted, not the regulations of their original batch. Same applies to students returning from gap year.

FFCS v4.0

Students re-joining after a break of study or detention follow the regulations into which they're readmitted. Programme migration to a different programme is permitted at the end of Year 1, subject to circulars issued by Dean Academics.

41 / LATERAL

Lateral Entry Provisions

AR-23

A dedicated section governs Lateral Entry students (admitted directly to Year 2 from 2024-25 onwards):

  • Duration: 3 to 6 academic years.
  • Total credits: 120 (vs 160 for Regular).
  • Honours: same 15 additional credits.
  • Promotion from III → IV year: 40% credits-cleared rule applied at Sem V.
  • All other rules of the B.Tech (Regular) regulation apply.
FFCS v4.0

FFCS v4.0 does not include a separate B.Tech Lateral Entry chapter in the supplied text. Lateral entry admissions, where applicable, would follow VIT's specific admission criteria for that programme.

42 / RECOGNITION

Special Recognition (Honours Club / Distinction)

AR-23

Recognition through the formal "First Class with Distinction" class designation, awarded only if the student crossed CGPA 7.5 without any supplementary appearance.

FFCS v4.0

Honours Club: students who maintain CGPA ≥ 9.25, with no F grade and no record of being debarred, are admitted. Benefits include:

  • Preference for university-sponsored conferences/workshops.
  • Certificate of Merit.
  • Possible cash award/scholarship for following semesters.

Admission is withdrawn the moment a condition is breached.

AR-23 rewards excellence through the degree-certificate Class; FFCS does it through the Honours Club with material benefits during the programme itself.

44 / AMENDMENTS

Modification of Regulations

AR-23

Universities may amend regulations/syllabi at any time. Changes apply to all students on rolls from the notified date. Final interpretive authority: Vice-Chancellor.

FFCS v4.0

Academic Council holds authority to add, delete, or modify the regulations. Each version supersedes all previous versions and circulars. Final interpretation: Academic Council.

45 / TRANSCRIPTS

Grade Sheets & Transcripts

AR-23

Standard semester grade sheet with subject-wise letter grade, grade points, SGPA, CGPA. The transcript carries the Class designation (Distinction, First, Second, Pass). The CGPA-to-percentage formula (CGPA-0.5)×10 is the official conversion.

FFCS v4.0

Semester Grade Sheet lists every registered course including F/N/W/U/P, substituted, re-registered, audit, grade-improvement attempts — a complete academic activity log per semester. The Consolidated Grade Sheet shows only the CGPA (no class). Minor/Honours courses are listed under separate headings with their own basket-level CGPA mention.

Additional courses crediting beyond curriculum requirement become "Additional" and incur fees at end of programme.

The FFCS Grade Sheet is essentially a complete academic-history ledger: every attempt, every withdrawal, every audit is visible. AR-23 keeps the Grade Sheet cleaner but the cost is reduced transparency about course-level history.

46 / INDUCTION

Induction Programme

AR-23

A 3-week mandatory induction before the start of Semester 1, with explicit modules listed:

  • Physical Activities — Sports, Yoga, Meditation, Plantation
  • Career Counselling, Human Values & Professional Ethics
  • Orientation to all branches, then to the admitted branch
  • Proficiency Modules & Productivity Tools
  • Assessment on basic aptitude and math; Remedial Training
  • Communication Skills (LSRW)
  • Concepts of Programming

Attendance per AICTE norms applies to induction.

FFCS v4.0

FFCS does not detail a formal multi-week induction in the regulation. Bridge courses (English, Programming, etc.) are assigned in Semester 1 itself based on a screening test, serving a similar purpose but integrated into the regular schedule.

47 / HOTs

Higher-Order Thinking Questions

AR-23

AR-23 specifies that objective papers "shall be prepared in line with the quality of competitive examinations questions" but does not give a numerical proportion of HOTs across the question paper.

FFCS v4.0

All CAT and FAT papers must carry at least 80% HOTs-based questions. Open-book CAT-II must be 100% HOTs. The system explicitly targets analysis, evaluation, and synthesis above recall.

48 / LOAD

Credit Load Per Semester

AR-23

Per-semester load is implicitly fixed by the branch course-structure (typically 19–21 credits per semester in Year 1, totalling 160 over 8 semesters). There is no explicit minimum/maximum credit cap that a student can dial.

FFCS v4.0

Explicit caps for regular semesters:

  • Maximum: 27 credits
  • Minimum: 16 credits
  • Average: 23 credits (programme-design target)

Below 16 only allowed in the last registration of a programme. CGPA < 4 → capped at 16. Summer/weekend semesters: no cap.

This is the central "flexibility lever" of FFCS — the ability to dial credit load up (fast pace) or down (slow pace, recover-from-failure pace) explicitly per semester. AR-23, by structure, doesn't expose that lever.

One way to see it: AR-23 is an NEP 2020-modernised version of a classical Indian engineering curriculum — structured, AICTE-aligned, community-service-conscious, with stronger exit ramps and a hard line on class designations. FFCS v4.0 is a credit-system that asks the student to actively own their academic plan — flexible pacing, relative grading, granular re-registration, broader degree coverage, and a stronger international-mobility scaffolding.

A student moving between the two systems would notice: the FFCS pass bar is higher (50% vs 40%); the FFCS internal weight is higher (60% vs 30%); the FFCS Grade Sheet is more transparent; AR-23's class designation gives a quick prestige label on the degree that FFCS deliberately abolishes; and the FFCS slot-based, choice-rich semester is structurally different from AR-23's branch-prescribed semester.
