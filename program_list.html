§ 01 · Foundations

What "CS + Domain" actually means

These programs pair a complete computer-science spine — algorithms, data structures, operating systems, databases, machine learning — with deep coursework in one industry vertical. The ratio is roughly 55–60% CS, 25% domain, 15% mathematics and humanities, with an industry partner shaping the domain electives.

The pattern is global. Universities noticed that hiring managers in fintech, healthcare, manufacturing and consulting were tired of training fresh CS graduates from scratch on domain context. Programs like these collapse the first 1–2 years of on-the-job learning into the degree itself. The trade-off: slightly narrower lateral mobility compared to a generalist CS degree, but a much faster ramp into specialized roles.

"To produce engineers who understand both technology and business — ideal for roles in the digital and IT services economy." — TCS, on why it co-designed the CSBS curriculum

The remainder of this document walks through thirteen specific programs across four domains, plus the two grading systems they use. Begin with the grading sections — every program description that follows refers back to them.

§ 02 · Grading Systems

The Indian 10-point system (AICTE & UGC)

Nearly every engineering college in India — IITs, NITs, private universities, state colleges — follows a 10-point Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) scale mandated by AICTE and UGC. Letter grades are tied to grade points, and grade points are credit-weighted into per-semester and overall averages.

The grade scale

The letter grades and their associated points map to mark ranges as follows. The bands are widely used though some institutions tweak them by a few percentage points.

Letter Grade Point Marks Range Descriptor Status
O 10 90 – 100 Outstanding Pass
A+ 9 80 – 89 Excellent Pass
A 8 70 – 79 Very Good Pass
B+ 7 60 – 69 Good Pass
B 6 55 – 59 Above Average Pass
C 5 50 – 54 Average Pass
P 4 40 – 49 Pass Pass (bare)
F 0 < 40 Fail Reappear
Ab 0 — Absent Reappear

SGPA and CGPA — how they're calculated

SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) captures one semester. CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is the running average across all semesters completed. Both are credit-weighted, meaning a 4-credit course pulls harder on the average than a 2-credit lab.

Semester Grade Point Average SGPA = Σ (Cᵢ × Gᵢ) / Σ Cᵢ
  where Cᵢ = credits of course i, Gᵢ = grade point earned in course i
Cumulative Grade Point Average CGPA = Σ (Cⱼ × SGPAⱼ) / Σ Cⱼ
  where Cⱼ = total credits in semester j, SGPAⱼ = SGPA of semester j

A worked example

A second-semester CSBS student takes six courses with these credits and grades:

Course Credits (C) Grade Grade Point (G) C × G
Linear Algebra4A+936
Data Structures & Algorithms3O1030
Fundamentals of Economics2A816
Statistical Modeling3B+721
Business Communication II4A832
Principles of Electronics3B618
Totals19——153

SGPA = 153 / 19 = 8.05. That's a "Very Good" overall semester. If subsequent semesters average around the same, CGPA stabilises around 8.0 — the threshold most premium recruiters look for.

Converting CGPA to percentage

There is no single conversion formula. Universities use different multipliers, often listed on the back of the grade sheet.

  • CGPA × 10 — most direct, used by many state technical universities
  • CGPA × 9.5 — common in central universities and CBSE-aligned institutions
  • (CGPA − 0.5) × 10 — used by AKTU (Lucknow) and several UP institutions
  • (CGPA − 0.75) × 10 — Anna University regulations
  • VTU formula: (SGPA − 0.75) × 10 — Karnataka state engineering

Pass criteria and degree class

Most engineering universities require:

  • Grade P (4) or higher in every course to pass that course
  • SGPA ≥ 5.0 at the end of each semester
  • CGPA ≥ 5.0 to be awarded the B.Tech degree
  • First Class with Distinction — final CGPA ≥ 7.5 (some universities: ≥ 7.0)
  • First Class — final CGPA ≥ 6.5 but < 7.5
  • Second Class — final CGPA ≥ 5.0 but < 6.5

ATKT — the backlog rule

"Allowed To Keep Term" rules govern how many failed papers a student may carry forward while still registering for the next semester. The typical cap is 2 backlogs per semester; exceeding it freezes promotion until the student clears arrears via supplementary exams. Some institutions cap total active backlogs over the program at 4–6.

Attendance

AICTE mandates a minimum 75% attendance per course; up to 25% can be condoned on medical grounds. Falling below the threshold usually bars a student from sitting the end-semester exam in that course.

Recruiters' CGPA floor
Tier-1 service companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture) typically require CGPA ≥ 6.5 with no active backlogs. Premium firms (Microsoft, Amazon, Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan) usually expect 7.5+. PSU recruitment via GATE (BHEL, ONGC, NTPC) requires 60–65% in graduation, which translates to roughly CGPA 6.5–7.0 depending on the formula used.
§ 03 · Grading Systems

The US 4.0 GPA system (Purdue, CMU, UPenn, JHU)

American universities use a 4.0 Grade Point Average scale with letter grades broken into thirds (A, A−, B+, B, B−...). The math is the same as Indian SGPA — credit-weighted average of grade points — but the scale and thresholds differ.

The grade scale

Letter Grade Point Marks Range Descriptor
A4.093 – 100Excellent
A−3.790 – 92Excellent
B+3.387 – 89Good
B3.083 – 86Good
B−2.780 – 82Good
C+2.377 – 79Satisfactory
C2.073 – 76Satisfactory
C−1.770 – 72Below Average
D1.060 – 69Poor (bare pass)
F0.0< 60Fail

Computing GPA

Grade Point Average GPA = Σ (Creditsᵢ × Grade Pointᵢ) / Σ Creditsᵢ
  Term GPA covers one term; Cumulative GPA covers the entire program.

Honors thresholds

US universities award Latin honors at graduation based on cumulative GPA. Thresholds vary by school but the rough convention is:

  • Summa cum laude — GPA ≥ 3.9 (top ~5% of class at competitive schools)
  • Magna cum laude — GPA ≥ 3.75 (top ~10%)
  • Cum laude — GPA ≥ 3.5 (top ~25%)
  • Dean's List (per term) — usually GPA ≥ 3.5 in that term with full course load

Indian-to-US conversion

For students applying to US graduate schools from Indian B.Tech programs, the approximate equivalents are:

Official credential evaluation services like WES (World Education Services) do this conversion authoritatively for US graduate applications.

§ 05 · Domain Group B

CS + Business Systems

This is the most widely available "CS + domain" pattern in India because TCS designed a model curriculum and licensed it to dozens of colleges. The "business" part isn't a single industry — it's business communication, accounting, economics, operations research, and management — preparing graduates for IT-services roles that need both code and client-facing business judgment.

Program 04 · India · UG · 4 years

B.Tech in Computer Science & Business Systems (CSBS)

Curriculum by Tata Consultancy Services, offered at 100+ AICTE-approved colleges
Duration4 yrs / 8 sem
Total Credits180 credits
Per Semester~22 credits
Industry PartnerTCS (faculty trained)

CSBS was created by TCS in partnership with the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, and approved by AICTE in 2019. It's listed in the official AICTE engineering course nomenclature, so the degree is recognized identically to a regular B.Tech. The premise: graduates know core CS (algorithms, OS, DBMS, networks, ML) and have working understanding of business communication, financial accounting, economics, management, and design thinking.

Grading

Standard AICTE 10-point CGPA. Each course uses Continuous Assessment (typically 30–40% — quizzes, mid-sems, assignments, lab work) + End Semester Exam (60–70%). Labs may carry their own grades. Projects in semesters VII and VIII are graded on review presentations, written report, and viva.

To graduate: complete 180 credits, CGPA ≥ 5.0, no active F or Ab grades. Honors track (Distinction) requires CGPA ≥ 7.5 at most colleges, ≥ 8.0 at top-tier institutions.

Career outcomes
  • TCS Digital / TCS Code Vita roles directly (CSBS grads come pre-aligned with the TCS skill bundle)
  • IT consulting at Accenture, Deloitte, Infosys, Cognizant, Capgemini
  • Business analyst roles at investment banks & consulting firms
  • Pivot to MBA after 2–3 years of experience (CSBS background reduces the "tech-to-business" friction)
Program 05 · India · Integrated UG+PG · 5 years

Integrated B.Tech (CSBS) + MBA

Pondicherry University, Department of Banking Technology
Duration5 yrs (integrated)
OutputB.Tech + MBA
FrameworkNEP 2020
Discipline HostBanking Technology / Mgmt

Launched under NEP 2020, this is one of the more distinctive options because it's hosted by Pondicherry University's Department of Banking Technology in the School of Management — combining the TCS CSBS curriculum with banking-specific electives and an MBA capstone. Graduates exit with both an engineering and management qualification, which is unusual at 5 years.

Structure

Grading

10-point CGPA throughout. The MBA year uses additional assessment via case studies (15–25% weight), group presentations, and a final dissertation. Honors are awarded based on cumulative CGPA across all 10 semesters.

§ 06 · Domain Group C

CS + Manufacturing, MES & Industrial Informatics

If you specifically want Manufacturing Execution Systems (MES) and Industry 4.0 — IIoT, shop-floor data, ERP integration, smart factories — this is the cleanest match. The US program below covers MES integration head-on; in India, you'd typically reach the same destination through Industrial Engineering plus electives.

§ 07 · Domain Group D

CS + Healthcare & Biomedical

The healthcare-CS intersection has the deepest research history of any vertical here. Programs split into three flavours: applied health informatics (managing EHRs, hospital systems), biomedical engineering with computational specialisation (devices, signal processing), and biomedical AI (diagnostic ML, imaging).

Strong in data science, AI, and computational biology. Research-heavy program often linked to applications in healthcare and biomedical engineering. Notable degrees:

More accessible than CMU/UPenn/JHU; offers a robust foundation for cross-disciplinary CS-healthcare careers. Programs include Biology, Chemistry, Biomedical Engineering, and Pre-Professional Health Sciences. Strong emphasis on hands-on lab work and internships.

Grading (all three institutions)

Standard US 4.0 GPA. UPenn and JHU enforce minimum 2.0 for graduation; honors thresholds at JHU: General Honors (≥ 3.5), Departmental Honors (≥ 3.5 + thesis), University Honors (≥ 3.7 + thesis).

If your interest is research rather than industry, this category is the gold standard in India. IIIT Hyderabad's dual degrees combine a B.Tech with a research-based Master's in a paired discipline — and crucially, the institute is located in Hyderabad itself.

IIIT-H pioneered the "B.Tech + MS by Research" dual-degree model in India. Unlike standard B.Tech (where final-year project is a token effort), the dual-degree route makes research central from year 2 onwards. Students are paired with a research lab and produce a master's thesis. Graduates are placed in roles split between top tech companies and PhD admissions at global universities.

Curriculum structure
  • Semesters 1–5: Heavy CS core (programming, algorithms, data structures, formal methods, DB, operating systems, computer architecture) + basic science/domain courses + mathematics + humanities
  • Semesters 6–8: Advanced CS electives + deeper specialization courses in the paired domain. By semester 8, domain courses ≥ CS courses
  • Semesters 9–10: Full-time MS thesis research at one of IIIT-H's labs (Computer Vision, NLP, IR, Speech Recognition, Cognitive Sciences, Data Sciences, Databases, Robotics, IoT, Spatial Informatics, Quantum Computing, Theoretical CS, Information Security, CADD, Bioinformatics, Systems Biology, Computational Neuroscience, Software Engineering, Cloud, Music Research, Hardware Acceleration)
Eligibility

Admission via the UGEE (IIIT-H's own entrance exam) for direct-entry dual-degree, or via JEE Main for the 4-year B.Tech route. For UGEE: 60% in PCM in 10+2. Lateral entry (LEEE) after 2nd year of B.Tech is also possible.

Grading

IIIT-H uses a 10-point CGPA scale identical in structure to the AICTE norm, but with stricter assessment — the institute uses relative grading, so even mark cutoffs shift based on class performance. Research thesis (semesters 9–10) is graded by an internal-external committee on novelty, technical depth, and publication potential. Many students publish in top conferences (NeurIPS, CVPR, ACL, etc.) during the master's phase.

Career outcomes (recent batches)
  • Highest reported B.Tech CSE package: ₹1.28 crore per annum; average ₹31.98 LPA
  • Dual-degree grads disproportionately go into PhDs at MIT, Stanford, CMU, Berkeley, Oxford
  • Research engineer roles at Microsoft Research, Google Research, Adobe Research, Amazon Science
  • Research-heavy startups in NLP (Indian languages), drug discovery, fintech
For Hyderabad-based candidates
IIIT-H is the strongest cross-domain CS option physically in Hyderabad. The CND (Computational Natural Sciences) and CLD (Computational Linguistics) streams in particular have no real equivalent elsewhere in India. Note that admissions are highly competitive — UGEE selects roughly 100 students per year combined across all streams.

§ 10 · Closing thoughts

How to choose

Use this rough decision tree based on your strongest signal: career destination, geographic constraint, or learning style.

If you know your industry, pick the deepest specialist

  • Banking / fintech in India → MIT Manipal CS & FinTech (deepest), or Karnavati if Paytm is your target employer
  • IT services + consulting → CSBS at any reputable college; BVRIT or VBIT if you want Hyderabad-local
  • Smart manufacturing / MES → Purdue if you can go to the US; otherwise B.Tech Industrial Engineering at an IIT/NIT with electives in Industry 4.0
  • Health informatics / biomedical AI → CMU's MS AIE-BME (if you have an undergrad in engineering and want a PG); otherwise JHU or UPenn at UG

If you're geographically constrained to Hyderabad

  • IIIT Hyderabad dual degree — most distinctive option, research-heavy
  • BVRIT CSBS in Narsapur (commutable) — TCS-aligned, industry-oriented
  • VBIT CSBS in Hyderabad city — similar value, different campus culture

If you're not sure yet

  • Pick CSBS — it's the broadest "CS + something" net, keeps general SDE options open, and the business modules don't lock you in
  • Avoid hyper-specialist programs (Paytm-branded fintech, single-vendor MES) at age 17–18 unless you're certain — they narrow lateral mobility
  • Watch fees vs ROI — a ₹22L MIT Manipal degree is worth it if you'll get fintech roles at ₹15–20 LPA; a ₹6L CSBS at a state college can give the same TCS placement at lower risk

Common pitfalls

  • Don't confuse "CSBS" with "CSE" — the former is the TCS-collaborative business-systems track; the latter is plain computer science engineering. They're both AICTE-recognised but lead to different roles.
  • Vendor-branded programs (Paytm, FIS) lock the curriculum to one company's stack. Great for placement at that company; weaker signal elsewhere.
  • Integrated 5-year B.Tech+MBA programs save one year but cost flexibility — you can't switch your MBA to a different school without losing the integration.
  • "Honours" tracks usually require an additional 18–24 credits beyond the base 160. Verify the workload before committing.
The best program isn't the one with the fanciest brochure — it's the one whose curriculum matches the work you actually want to do in five years. Look at the elective list, not the eyebrow-raising acronyms.
